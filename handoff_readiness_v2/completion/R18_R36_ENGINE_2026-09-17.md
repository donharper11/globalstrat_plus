## 1. R18 — a product retired `end_of_round` sells through that round

### What was wrong

`rd_processing._process_product_retires` ran the *same three statements* for
both timings: status `retired`, `retired_round` set, and **every**
`TeamProductMarket` row deactivated. Retirement runs at `advance_round:780`,
before adoption (`:810`) and revenue (`:817`), and both the demand path
(`preference_engine._get_team_products_in_market`) and readiness require
`status='active'` **and** `is_active=True`. So an `end_of_round` retirement
took the product off sale in the round the team chose to sell through.

With identical market timing, the only difference left was fire-sale recovery
— `costs.py:881`, **50%** for `end_of_round` against **25%** for `immediate`.
`immediate` was therefore **strictly dominated**: worse recovery, no
compensating benefit, and no reason for any team ever to choose it. That is the
defect R18 was issued to remove.

### What changed, and where

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `backend/core/engine/rd_processing.py` | `_process_product_retires` now applies the **`immediate`** timing only. New `_retire_now` helper holds the shared three statements. New `apply_end_of_round_retirements(context)` applies the deferred timing. |
| `backend/core/engine/advance_round.py` | New **Step 16.5**, immediately before Step 17 marks the round processed: calls `apply_end_of_round_retirements(context)`. |

**The placement is the ruling.** The deferred step is the *last deterministic
mutation of Phase 1* — after adoption, revenue, costs, financials, the
performance index, coherence, the agent cycle and the leaderboard. Everything
that constitutes the round therefore saw the product as it was all round, which
is what "sells through that round" means; from the next round it is retired and
off sale.

`immediate` is unchanged and still stops sales at once, in `process_rd`, before
adoption. The two timings now differ in **market timing as well as recovery**,
so the authored 50%/25% rates stand as authored and neither option dominates.

### The determinism envelope does not move

`complete_manifest` runs after `_run_phase_1` returns
(`advance_round.py:347`), so the retired state is snapshotted inside the
round's hashed output — `team_product` and `team_product_market` are both
enumerated sections (`manifest_sections.py:443,453`). That is V2-043's point
preserved rather than reverted: what R18 supersedes is only *when* the
deactivation happens, not whether it is inside the envelope.

**No section and no field changes**, only the values inside existing ones.
`MANIFEST_SCHEMA_VERSION` stays at **6** (`manifest_version.py:23`,
unmodified — confirmed by `git diff --stat`, which lists no
`manifest_version.py` and no `manifest_schema_v*.json`).

### One thing I did *not* change, and it is an owner question

`costs.calculate_retirement_costs` computes the write-off and the fire-sale
recovery from the **previous** round's unsold units
(`prev_round = current_round - 1`, `costs.py:898`). Under R18 an `end_of_round`
product sells through its retirement round, so its genuinely stranded stock is
arguably **this** round's unsold units, not last round's.

I left the basis exactly as authored, for two reasons. First, R18 rules on
market timing and says the recovery rates stand; it does not speak to the
basis, and changing it would alter `immediate` as well — a competitive rule
change no ruling ordered. Second, it is not even available at that point in the
pipeline: `calculate_retirement_costs` runs at `advance_round:840` and this
round's `RoundResultProductMarket` rows are not written until
`generate_financial_statements` at `:856`.

**Recorded as a question for the rules owner, not as a defect I worked around.**
If the owner wants the write-off to follow the sell-through, it is a reordering
plus a basis change, and it needs its own ruling.

## 2. R36 — the organisational-structure charge moves into the engine

### What was wrong

`cc32b_views.py:148-149` did `team.cash_on_hand -= new_structure.transition_cost;
team.save()` at request time. The charge existed in **no calculator** — not
`funding_need.decision_outlays`, not `rd_costs.budget_assessment`, not the
engine. The money left the business at click time while every committed-spend,
projected-cash and Finance figure the team is shown ignored it, the equity
funding rule never counted it, and no path restored it, so reopening a round
left the cash gone while the decision it paid for could still be changed.

### What changed, and where

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `backend/core/services/funding_need.py` | New **`org_transition_charge(team, current_round)`** — the single shared calculator. New `org_structure` line in `decision_outlays`, computed **before** the `submission is None` guard. |
| `backend/core/engine/costs.py` | `calculate_operating_expenses` books the same figure through the same function into `strategy_expense`, and the **parity assertion is widened** to cover it. |
| `backend/core/services/rd_costs.py` | `committed_outlay(submission, team=None)` gains an `org_transition` line; `budget_assessment` adds it to `committed_total`. |
| `backend/core/views/cc32b_views.py` | The cash deduction is **gone**. Affordability now runs through `budget_assessment` inside a transaction that rolls the switch back on refusal (`_SwitchUnaffordable`). |

### No new hashed field — R34's principle

The charge is **derived from state that already exists**: the switch is
recognised by `adopted_round == current_round` **and**
`transitioning_from__isnull=False` on the team's `TeamOrganizationalStructure`
row, and priced from `current_structure.transition_cost`.

That matters for three reasons, all deliberate:

* the charge and the decision that caused it cannot disagree, because there is
  one row and it *is* the decision;
* a round with no switch costs nothing, and a switch made in an earlier round
  is not charged again;
* **no field is added to a hashed section**, so the envelope is unchanged and
  `MANIFEST_SCHEMA_VERSION` stays at 6. `team_org_structure` is already a
  hashed section (`manifest_sections.py:482`), so a new column there would have
  forced v7 exactly as the paid-research section did. **The existing audit
  record stays exactly as it was** — `record_decision_event(...,
  'change_org_structure', ...)` is untouched.

Because the figure is recomputed from the stored row, re-resolving a round
yields the same amount rather than a cumulative one.

### The one-calculator invariant, shown in both places

**Place 1 — the shared calculator / funding rule**, `funding_need.py`:

```python
lines['org_structure'] = org_transition_charge(team, current_round)
if submission is None:
    return lines
```

**Place 2 — the engine**, `costs.calculate_operating_expenses`:

```python
from core.services.funding_need import org_transition_charge
org_transition = org_transition_charge(team, current_round)
strategy_expense += org_transition
```

Both call **the same function**, so neither can price the switch differently.

Both compute it **outside the submission guard**, and that is load-bearing: a
structure switch writes no decision row of its own, so a team can switch in a
round it never otherwise submitted in. Had `decision_outlays` computed it after
its early return while the engine computed it unconditionally, the two would
diverge in exactly that case.

**Place 3 — affordability**, `rd_costs.committed_outlay`, so the team's own
committed spend, projected cash and Finance figures include it (these all read
`committed_total`, per V2-057):

```python
lines['org_transition'] = org_transition_charge(
    team or submission.team, submission.round.round_number)
```

**The invariant is enforced, not merely satisfied.** The existing parity
assertion did not cover the new line, so I widened it:

```python
_shared = (_outlays['rd'] + _outlays['platform_capex']
           + _outlays['marketing'] + _outlays['research']
           + _outlays['org_structure'])
_engine = (rd_expense + platform_capex + marketing_expense
           + research_expense + org_transition)
if _shared != _engine:
    raise AssertionError(...)
```

`test_the_parity_assertion_covers_the_new_line` proves the widening bites: it
patches `decision_outlays` to forget the line and asserts the round **stops**
with an `AssertionError` rather than resolving with the two sides pricing
different things.

### Reopening and re-processing

Nothing but the switch is written at click time, so **a reopened round has
nothing to unwind** — the money never moved. This is the shape of the repair
rather than an added mechanism, and it matters because no unwind path exists to
add it to: `round_control.py`, `advance_round.py` and `lifecycle.py` contain no
`cash_on_hand` write at all. Charging at resolution makes an unresolved round
cost nothing by construction, and undoing the decision removes the charge with
the row (`test_undoing_the_decision_removes_the_charge`).

### One behaviour change beyond the literal ruling, flagged

The view now **creates the round's `DecisionSubmission`** if the team has none
(`get_or_create`, `status='draft'`), exactly as a research purchase does
(`research_reports.py:772`). Without it `budget_assessment` has nothing to
assess. It is the same pattern already in the tree for the same reason, but it
is a behaviour change and I am naming it rather than burying it.

## 5. What evidence this invalidates

1. **`core.tests.test_product_retirement.ProductRetirementTests.test_end_of_round_retirement_deactivates_product_market`** — the V2-043 regression. It asserted an `end_of_round` retirement leaves the product *not on sale immediately*, which R18 reverses for that timing. **Replaced**, not deleted: the module now pins both timings separately. V2-043's substance (the rows must not be left stale inside the hashed envelope) is still asserted, at the round's end.
2. **Any stored replay or manifest evidence covering a round that contains an `end_of_round` retirement or an organisational-structure switch.** Both change *what* and *when* state moves, so `output_sha256` moves for such rounds. Earlier replay evidence stands for its own commit and not for `f1b8ed2`. R18 was issued requiring a focused replay regression; that regression is **not** delivered here (see §6).

5. **Not invalidated:** CRV2-01's canonical-serialisation, envelope-enumeration and iteration-order evidence. No section, field or ordering changed, and run 4 re-proves it on this commit.

## 6. What I could not verify

* **No full suite.** GSP-CRV2-09 owns it and it is the next gate. I ran 361
  tests across 13 modules chosen by their dependence on what I changed.
* **No replay regression, and R18 explicitly asks for one.** `determinism_fixture.py`
  cannot resolve a round at head — that is **V2-116**, open and not mine — so
  the CRV2-01 fixture could not be used to produce a before/after replay.
  `v6_envelope_fixture.py` is noted in the read-through as a working pattern.
  **This is the single largest gap in this handoff.**
* **"Sells in its retirement round" is proved through eligibility, not through a
  resolved round's `units_sold`.** I assert the product keeps `status='active'`
  and `is_active=True` through retirement processing *and* that
  `preference_engine._get_team_products_in_market` still returns it — which is
  the query that turns an offered product into demand. I did **not** run a full
  `_run_phase_1` with an `end_of_round` retirement and assert a non-zero
  `units_sold`. The eligibility proof is sound and localises the change, but it
  is not the end-to-end figure, and I am not claiming it is.
* **No browser pass.** `CorporateStrategyPage.js` is untouched and still
  displays `transition_cost`, but I did not build or click it.
* **The refusal sentence in `cc32b_views.py` is English-only.** I changed its
  wording (it now names committed spend against cash, because that is the rule
  now applied). Every other error string in this view is already English-only
  and outside the bilingual catalogue, so I kept it consistent rather than
  introducing a half-translated surface. **If GSP-CRV2-12's standard binds this
  view, this string and its neighbours need cataloguing — flagged, not done.**
* **A locked submission does not block a structure switch.** The view's gate is
  round-open, as before; a research purchase additionally refuses after lock.
  I did not add that gate — it is a behaviour change no ruling ordered — but
  the asymmetry is real and worth a decision.
* **`apply_org_structure_modifiers` decrements `transition_rounds_remaining`
  every resolution**, so re-processing a round would decrement twice.
  Pre-existing and untouched, but adjacent to this work and I noticed it.
* `test_operator_concurrency` was not run (hundreds of threaded races; it
  belongs to the full-suite gate).
