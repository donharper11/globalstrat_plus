# GSP-CRV2-12 — Player-facing language sweep

**Observes:** `specs/STANDING-DISCIPLINE.md`, `handoffs/EXECUTION_PROTOCOL.md`
**Source:** `handoff_readiness_v2/RULES_AND_CALIBRATION_ASSESSMENT.md` Part C
**Owner:** product/UX engineer with instructional-design review
**Sequence:** after GSP-CRV2-10 and 11 have landed their user-visible strings.
Sweeping before they land means sweeping twice.

## Objective

Every message the platform shows a participant during play reads as business
language written for an executive-education audience — not as the platform's
internal vocabulary. A student who hits a limit learns what the rule is and what
to do next; they do not learn a column name.

## What is wrong today

Refusals a student can hit right now, verbatim:

- `retail_price must be > 0.`
- `promotion_budget must be >= 0.`, `rd_budget must be >= 0.`, and ~19 more of
  this exact shape generated by `decision_limits.non_negative_message`
- `target_market_ids must be a non-empty list.`
- `Product "X" targets market 7 where team has no active presence.` — a raw row id
- `Platform has no team_id.`
- `No SimulationState found for this instance.`
- `instance_id is required.`, `game_id and team_id required`
- `Validation failed.` — on decision submit, with no indication of what failed

**And the sharper problem for this audience: backend messages are not localised
at all.** `core/utils/localization.py` resolves `*_zh` fields on *scenario
content* only. Every validation error, instructor alert and system message is
English-only. A zh-CN team plays a Chinese interface and receives English
refusals at exactly the moment something has gone wrong. The frontend catalogue
is by contrast in good shape — `locales/en.json` and `locales/zh-CN.json` are
within two lines of each other — which makes the backend gap conspicuous.

## Stage 1 — inventory

Build the inventory from the code, not from clicking. Every string that can
reach a participant or an instructor:

- DRF `ValidationError` messages across `core/serializers/`
- `errors.append(...)` on the decision submit path (`core/views/decisions.py`)
- `{'error': ...}` / `{'detail': ...}` response bodies across `core/views/`
- `InstructorAlert` titles, details and teaching notes
  (`core/engine/instructor_alerts.py`)
- Persona, narrative, briefing and coaching output that is templated rather
  than model-generated (`core/engine/narratives.py`, `briefing.py`,
  `core/services/persona_engine.py`)
- Event and challenge text surfaced to teams
- Frontend literals not routed through `t()` — several pages carry hardcoded
  English (`GameDashboard.js`, `CorporateStrategyPage.js`,
  `FinancialReportsPage.js` among them)
- Any string GSP-CRV2-10 and 11 added; take their handover list rather than
  rediscovering it

Classify each row: **participant-facing** / **instructor-facing** /
**operator-or-log-only**. Operator and log strings are out of scope and should
stay technical — an engineer reading a log needs the field name. Say so
explicitly per row so the sweep's boundary is reviewable.

## Stage 2 — the standard

Write it down once, in the evidence directory, and apply it uniformly:

- Name the business object, not the database column: "unit price", not
  `retail_price`; "target markets", not `target_market_ids`.
- Never show an internal id. `market 7` becomes the market's name.
- Say what the rule is **and** what to do next. `Marketing spend ($4,200,000)
  exceeds your marketing budget ($3,000,000). Reduce spend or raise the
  marketing budget in Budget Allocation.`
- State money, percentages and rounds the way the rest of the interface does.
- A refusal at submit names the decision area, so a team can navigate to it.
  `Validation failed.` is not a message.
- Warnings and refusals are visibly different: a warning is advice on an
  accepted action, a refusal means nothing was saved. BECSR's
  `budget.over_budget_warning` and `pricing.price_status` are the reference for
  wording a rule once and letting every surface reuse it — three surfaces
  describing one rule three ways is the defect being avoided.
- The rule is worded in **one place** and imported. Do not fix the same sentence
  in two files.

## Stage 3 — bilingual parity

- Route participant-facing backend messages through the localisation the rest of
  the platform uses, so a zh-CN participant gets Chinese refusals.
- Any message with a `zh` gap is a finding, not a nice-to-have: this platform is
  bilingual by design and a competition will have zh-CN teams.
- Verify every message that interpolates a value (money, dates, counts) reads
  correctly in both languages — interpolation order is where translated strings
  usually break.
- Frontend literals move into the catalogue with both languages supplied.

## Stage 4 — walk it

Not a click-everything pass. One student walkthrough and one instructor
walkthrough on a seeded game, deliberately triggering each **class** of message:
a refused decision, a warning, a lock, a deadline, a price-band alert and its
deadline adjustment (from CRV2-10), an event, a round result, an error. Capture
each in both languages. Reuse GSP-CRV2-08's completed game where it fits.

## Acceptance

- Every participant-facing string in the inventory is rewritten or explicitly
  and reviewably exempt.
- No internal identifier, column name, model name or raw id reaches a
  participant.
- Participant-facing backend messages are localised; EN/ZH parity is
  demonstrated, not asserted.
- Each message class is captured in both languages in the walkthrough record.
- A short authoring standard is checked in, so the next feature's strings do not
  regress the sweep.
- A static check fails the build on a new participant-facing string that is
  untranslated or names a model field. Without it this sweep is a snapshot.

## Evidence

`handoff_readiness_v2/evidence/player-language/` — the classified inventory
(before/after), the authoring standard, walkthrough captures in both languages,
and the static check with its failing and passing cases.

## Verification budget

Focused tests plus the two walkthroughs. No suite, no load, no matrices.
This handoff must not change engine behaviour; if a string change requires one,
it is a finding for the owning handoff rather than a repair made here.
